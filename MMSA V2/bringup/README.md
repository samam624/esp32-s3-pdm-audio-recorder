## 2026-10-20: signs of life (PASS)

`MMSA_V2_SignsOfLife/` prints chip, flash and PSRAM info over native USB, then a 1 Hz heartbeat. It drives no GPIOs.

Result on the first V2 board (COM5, MAC 7C:E8:B1:B9:0A:5C):
- ESP32-S3 (QFN56) rev v0.2, 240 MHz, USB-Serial/JTAG enumerates as VID 303A / PID 1001
- Flash: 8 MB quad, manufacturer 0x20, device 0x4017
- PSRAM: 8 MB **octal**, 1 MB write/read pattern test passed

## Build/flash

```
CLI="C:\Program Files\Arduino IDE\resources\app\lib\backend\resources\arduino-cli.exe"
FQBN="esp32:esp32:esp32s3:UploadSpeed=921600,USBMode=hwcdc,CDCOnBoot=cdc,CPUFreq=240,FlashMode=qio,FlashSize=8M,PartitionScheme=default_8MB,PSRAM=opi,DebugLevel=none"
"$CLI" compile -b "$FQBN" MMSA_V2_SignsOfLife
"$CLI" upload  -b "$FQBN" -p COM5 MMSA_V2_SignsOfLife
```

**Gotcha:** the PSRAM must be set to `PSRAM=opi`. With `PSRAM=enabled` (quad), the sketch reports "PSRAM: NOT FOUND". The V1 recorder FQBN uses `FlashSize=4M,PSRAM=disabled`. On V2, change it to `FlashSize=8M`. Keep `PSRAM=disabled` if the firmware reads the IO35 button (see below).

## 2026-10-20: REC button on IO35 (PASS, PSRAM disabled)

`MMSA_V2_ButtonTest/` reads IO35 (`INPUT_PULLUP`) and lights the IO40 LED while the button is held. Built with `PSRAM=disabled`, because IO35 is shared with the octal PSRAM. Result: 6 clean presses, idles high, no bounce double counts.

**The two results conflict:** PSRAM works only with `PSRAM=opi`, and the button works only with PSRAM disabled. Real firmware has to pick one (V1 picked no PSRAM), or the button needs a bodge wire to IO10/15/16.

## SD card test (prepared 2026-10-20)

`MMSA_V2_SD_Test/` is V1's `SD_Card_SPI_Diagnostic` with the card-detect pin removed (V2 has none), plus three additions:
- a 20 MHz write-speed test: 1 MB in 4 KB chunks, reports KB/s and the worst chunk time, then reads back and verifies
- REC button reruns the test
- REC LED shows the result: solid = pass, fast blink = fail

It runs on boot and prints a `RESULT:` line at the end. Build with `PSRAM=disabled`.

## 2026-10-20: first mics (MIC 0 + MIC 1 on data line IO5): audio OK, L/R duplicated

`MMSA_V2_MicTest/` is the live 8-slot level meter. IO41 brightness follows the loudest slot, and IO42 is a 1 Hz heartbeat (this also exercises those two LEDs). `MMSA_V2_MicDiag/` cycles through PDM configurations and measures how alike slots 0 and 1 are.

- Audio is present and reacts to sound: quiet room ~ -50 to -55 dBFS, loud ~ -14 dBFS.
- **Slot 0 and slot 1 (IO5 L/R) are the same stream:** identical in 100% of PCM samples (correlation 1.000), and **99.9–100% of raw PDM words**. Two mics on opposite clock edges can't be bit-identical, so one stream is reaching both edges. Likely causes:
  1. Both mics' SELECT (L/R) pins strapped to the same level, so both drive the same edge and fight.
  2. One mic isn't driving (bad joint or dead), and the data line holds its last bit through the other half-cycle.
- PCM with all 8 slots: accepted by the driver. Slots 2–7 (no mics fitted) sit at a constant -30935 with zero variance. It isn't yet proven that the hardware PDM→PCM filter really converts lines 1–3; confirm when a mic is fitted on IO6/7/8.
- RAW with all 8 slots: refused (`i2s_pdm_rx_set_clock: clock calculate failed`) at 3.072 MHz. RAW with 2 slots works. Not needed if the PCM route works on all lines.
- Mic is the **IMP34DT05** (datasheet saved). The flex schematic shows the LR straps are correct (MIC_L LR → GND, MIC_R LR → 1V8), so the duplicate means one mic isn't driving `DO`. See `reference/MIC_FLEX_MODULE.md` for suspects and the cover-one-port test.
- LED oddities during `MMSA_V2_MicTest` were not hardware: `MMSA_V2_LedProbe` found no bridges and each pin driving correctly, and a register dump showed all three LED pins configured correctly. After a clean serial reset the LEDs behaved. Suspect the board came up in a bad state after the flasher's reset; replug if LEDs freeze after a flash.

## 2026-10-20: corrections after an external code review

Taps aren't a valid per-mic test, because vibration travels through the flex to both mics.

- `MMSA_V2_MicDiag`: removed the 8-slot RAW experiment (always refused).
- The "snap turned IO40 off" observation is **unexplained**. IO40 is written only from the REC button, so it means LED misidentification, a REC button glitch, or coupling. The "bad boot after flash" explanation was a guess, not a finding.
- `MMSA_V2_SD_Test`: the append test now writes a unique token and confirms it reads back. Before, it only printed the file, which proved nothing about the new write.

## 2026-10-20: press test: one mic has an intermittent joint (hardware)

`MMSA_V2_PressTest/` is raw PDM on line IO5, comparing LEFT-edge vs RIGHT-edge words every 500 ms. Same firmware and config throughout.
- At rest: **99.8–100% identical** (one stream on the line).
- While the user pressed one mic (log uptime ~71–98 s): **18–20% identical (two distinct streams)**, steady the whole press.
- After release: back to ~100% within 2 s.
- Since the firmware can produce two distinct streams, the duplicate isn't a driver limitation. One mic's joint only connects under pressure. **Confirmed MIC 0**: holding MIC 0 alone gave a steady ~18.7% identical (two streams).
- If the bad joint is on MIC_R's **LR** pad, it's an especially good fit: an open LR falls back to the internal 200 kΩ pull-down, so MIC_R acts as a LEFT mic and both drive the same edge. An open DOUT/CLK/VDD on either mic fits too.
- Fix: reflow that mic (flux, hot air or hot plate). Remember the 3-reflow limit.
- Rework attempts on MIC 0 (left mic, LR=GND): two reflows plus pressing it down while the solder set. Still one stream at rest and two streams when pressed (left-edge density 0.43 vs 0.50 for the healthy mic). Next suspects: a cracked flex trace/via near MIC 0, or a damaged mic/pad (now at ST's 3-reflow limit). `MMSA_V2_PressTest` now lights the REC LED (IO40) while two streams are present, so the user can probe without serial.
- **Correction:** the press-test "two streams" was **not** MIC 0 connecting. The user found that a light finger touch anywhere on the flex between MIC 1 and the FPC (or a half-inserted FPC) triggers it. That's finger capacitance disturbing the undriven, floating left half-cycle of the DO line. Conclusion: MIC 0 doesn't drive the line at all. The test now also requires the left stream to toggle like real PDM before it counts as a mic.

## 2026-10-20: stereo SD recorder for listening tests

`MMSA_V2_StereoRecorder/` uses the same PDM config as V1's recorder (48 kHz, PCM stereo, CLK IO4, DIN IO5). REC toggles recording to `/MMSA_NNNN.wav` (LEFT = MIC 0, RIGHT = MIC 1), and the REC LED is on while recording. It reports DMA overflows on stop.
- Note: the user says the mics run from a **1.8 V LDO** fed from the 3V3 rail. On paper that's outside both datasheets: the 3.3 V CLK exceeds the mic's Vin abs max of Vdd+0.3 = 2.1 V (IMP34DT05 Table 5), and the 1.8 V DOUT high is below the ESP32-S3's VIH min of 0.75×3.3 = 2.48 V (WROOM-1 datasheet). The user reports the same 1.8 V setup worked in V1. Recorded as a risk to revisit (level shifting, or running the mics at 3.3 V; they're rated to 3.6 V), not as the cause of the MIC 0 issue.
- **Listening test (MMSA_0001.wav, 16.7 s, 0 DMA overflows):** the user hears MIC 0 on both channels, so **MIC 1 is the mic that isn't getting in.** That reverses my earlier MIC 0 attribution, and MIC 0 was reworked unnecessarily (now at 3 heat cycles). The bit-level data (one stream on the line) still holds; only *which* mic was wrong.
- **ROOT CAUSE (user, 2026-10-20): MIC 1 had no solder on any of its inner signal pads** (only the outer ring was joined). Reworked, and now **both mics on module 1 work** (user-confirmed by listening).
- Lessons for the remaining 6 mics: (1) LGA mics need solder on every inner pad. Pre-tin the pads or use paste/stencil, and don't rely on the ground ring wicking. (2) The "identical L/R slots" check reliably detects a missing mic on a line, but **it can't tell which mic**. Edge/slot-based attribution was wrong here. Identify the mic by listening with one port covered (`MMSA_V2_StereoRecorder`). (3) A finger on the DO trace disturbs the floating half-cycle and fakes a second stream; don't touch the flex during the test.
- **LED roles (user-defined 2026-10-20), now in `MMSA_V2_StereoRecorder`:** IO40 = record ready (solid while the SD card is mounted, live presence check plus auto-remount), IO41 = loudness meter (V1 level math, always active), IO42 = REC blink (400 ms while recording). Note this differs from V1, where 40 = REC, 41 = level, 42 = SD.

## 2026-10-20: buzzy 8-ch audio, analysis of `MMSA V2/data/*.wav`

Scripts in the session scratchpad (not in the repo): channel continuity, correlation, harmonic-comb buzz detector, band levels.
- **Interleaving is correct**: every 8-ch track is continuous audio (lag-1 autocorrelation ~0.98–1.00). The firmware isn't mixing channels.
- **No tonal buzz** (no harmonic series stands >~3.6 dB above neighbours in any file), so it isn't mains hum or a fixed-rate glitch.
- **Speech-band (300–3000 Hz) correlation:** stereo `MMSA_0004` mic0↔mic1 = 0.79. In `MMSA8_0002`, mic0↔mic1 = only 0.27 and all pairs 0.13–0.43. The mics agree much less than expected, which looks like added non-acoustic noise or errors.
- **Low-frequency excess:** 20–100 Hz is −28 to −34 dBFS in the 6-mic-era files (`MMSA1_0001` mono AND `MMSA8_0002`) versus −39 to −49 in the 2-mic stereo files, plus large sub-20 Hz energy (−14 to −25 dBFS) that the 35 Hz high-pass should be removing.
- **Key point:** `MMSA1_0001` (1 slot, 1 data line, the *old* firmware path) was recorded with 6 mics fitted and shares the 8-ch file's spectral signature. That points at a **hardware change between the 2-mic and 6-mic builds**, not at the 4-line firmware. Not a controlled comparison yet (different sounds recorded).
- **Leading hypothesis (from the datasheets):** the 3.3 V CLK into mics on a 1.8 V supply exceeds the IMP34DT05 Vin max of Vdd+0.3 = 2.1 V. Each mic's CLK protection diode conducts into the 1V8 rail, and with 6 mics the injected current can lift or modulate a rail that the LDO can't sink. Separately, the 1.8 V DOUT high is below the ESP32-S3's VIH min of 2.48 V, so marginal levels and rail noise can turn into PDM bit errors (heard as noise/distortion). Both scale with the number of mics, which fits "2 mics clean, 6 mics bad".
- Proposed tests: (A) listen to `MMSA1_0001`; if it's buzzy, the 4-line firmware is cleared. (B) Measure the 1V8 rail with the clock running (recorder) vs stopped (`MMSA_V2_MicLineShorts`); a rise with the clock running confirms injection. (C) Controlled re-record with the stereo recorder now that 6 mics are fitted. Candidate fixes: power the mics from 3V3 (IMP34DT05 is rated 1.6–3.6 V, which fixes both CLK and DOUT levels), or level-shift CLK/DOUT. A cheap firmware experiment: a lower PDM clock (32 kHz → 2.048 MHz) for more timing margin.
- **Correction / likely explanation:** the user heard mic0 hard left, mic1 hard right, and the others centered or slightly off. That's a player treating the plain 8-ch WAV as **7.1 surround** and downmixing (track 3 = center, **track 4 = mic3 = LFE, low-passed: sounds buzzy**, tracks 5–8 = surrounds), plus phasey summing of spaced mics. The buzz may well be a **playback artifact**; the per-track analysis found every track intact. The hardware-voltage hypothesis above is now unconfirmed and secondary. Added `MMSA V2/tools/split_wav.py` (one mono WAV per mic) and split `MMSA8_0002` into `data/split/` for per-mic listening.
- **`MMSA1_0002.wav` (mono, mic0, 6 mics fitted, board left undisturbed): user says it sounds perfect.** Numbers agree: sub-20 Hz −51.9, 20–100 Hz −49.9, 1–4 kHz −36.5 dBFS, DC −136, 1.6% sample jumps (cleaner than the 2-mic stereo files). **The hardware-voltage hypothesis is not supported**; the 6-mic build records cleanly. The rumble in `MMSA1_0001`/`MMSA8_0002` was most likely the recording conditions (handling), and the "buzzy" 8-ch playback the 7.1 downmix. Next: a fair 8-ch recording under the same conditions, split per mic.
